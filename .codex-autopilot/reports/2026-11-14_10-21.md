### B. Discovered Issues

**P2 — A cancelled queued Gateway send cannot return while another writer is blocked.** In `src/gateway/conn.go`, the inside-lock context recheck prevented late transmission, but `sync.Mutex.Lock` itself could not observe cancellation/deadlines. The caller could therefore wait for the earlier writer's socket timeout despite an expired context.

Evidence: `TestConnQueuedSendEndsWithContextWhileWriterIsBlocked` wraps the client connection and stalls one application write after a normal local WebSocket handshake. It starts a second send, confirms serialization, then cancels its context or lets a 100 ms deadline expire. For both opcode 4 commands and heartbeats, the original implementation remained blocked at a further 500 ms watchdog (four failures). Releasing the transport lets cleanup complete. This is a controlled synchronization probe, not a measurement of actual socket stalls or production latency.

**Resolved.** The queued wait now selects between acquiring the write gate and context completion; a recheck after acquisition covers simultaneously ready cancellation/access. Only the active holder may compress, alter heartbeat timing, set socket deadlines or write. A queued caller does not close the shared socket or interrupt the active writer. Already-started writes retain their existing five-second socket timeout and are outside this fix.
